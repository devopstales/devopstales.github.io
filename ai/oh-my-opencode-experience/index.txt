The promise holds for the right workload and taxes everything else: tripled tokens on single-window tasks, five-figure startup context, and at least one incident where a looping subagent billed hundreds unsupervised. ## Hands-On: Two Tasks, Two Verdicts

The orchestrator doesn&#39;t delegate on keyword alone. Without explicit framing (&#34;research first, plan in detail, delegate implementation, do direct work only when delegation is unreasonable&#34;), Sisyphus reads and writes everything itself — vanilla OpenCode wearing a heavier context window. `ultrawork` is prerequisite, not guarantee.

On a scoped multi-step build (CLI tool: read API spec, design, implement, test), explicit orchestration beat bare runs — Librarian-supplied docs contributed context the solo model missed. Research plus implementation plus design decisions is the shape this harness exists for. On a single-window document analysis task, the result matched the bare run identically at roughly triple the tokens: no parallelism to exploit, no specialist knowledge to import, pure overhead. Newcomers start in the [quickstart](/ai/oh-my-opencode-quickstart/); the roster mechanics live in the [agents deep dive](/ai/oh-my-opencode-agents/).

Weaker models amplify the effect in the wrong direction: orchestration noise hurts small models more than strong ones help them. Budget models want clean focused prompts; reserve the crew for research-heavy work where Explore and Librarian genuinely add information.

## Community Data: Benchmarks and Bills

A 120-combination community benchmark scored Ultrawork at 69.2% pass versus 73.1% for plain OpenCode — ten minutes slower, 96 requests against 27. &#34;Opus with more steps&#34;: resilient flagships barely notice harness differences, and single-window coding tasks often match or beat full orchestration bare. Startup overhead runs 15,000–25,000 tokens before any work begins (deferred tool loading pending); factor it into every pricing estimate.

The billing horror story is concrete: a Gemini subagent looped 809 turns over 3.5 hours (~$438) through three compounding failures — no per-subagent step cap, silent category routing onto an unchosen model, and cost display missing the over-200K-context 2x tier (shown at under half the real bill). Until circuit breakers land: audit category-to-model mappings, cap provider concurrency (Gemini at 1), and trust provider dashboards over displayed cost:

Counterweight wins are real: thousands of lint warnings cleared in a day via parallel Explore-plus-worker fleets, full-stack features shipped overnight under Ralph Loop execution, inherited-codebase reviews through read-only Oracle. Common thread: parallelism plus verifiable acceptance criteria. Lighter appetites suit slim forks and background-hooks-only harnesses over full orchestration.

## When Each Side Wins

Full stack with `ultrawork`: multi-file multi-layer scope, distinct research-plan-implement-verify phases, parallel context gathering while workers run, breadth you&#39;d otherwise babysit prompt by prompt. Vanilla OpenCode or leaner harness: single-window tasks, pure reasoning without research, budget models, unpredictable billing intolerance. The vanilla discipline — CLI workflows, conservative permissions, daily process — is covered in [OpenCode CLI practice](/ai/opencode-cli-in-practice/); base install and provider setup in the [quickstart](/ai/opencode-quickstart/).

## Summary

Power multiplier on the right shape, tax everywhere else. Point the crew at parallelizable multi-phase work with checkable outcomes, keep single-window reasoning bare, cap concurrency, watch real bills — and never fire-and-forget against Gemini until safeguards ship.
